Red palm weevil · date palms at 8 m · one frame per day

Six years after a 2 × 2 introduction

Four palms are infested on 1 January of year 1. Choose whether dead palms are replanted or left to stand, and whether to show six monitoring traps. Each simulation runs from day 1 to day 2190 and is drawn over an aerial image of a date-palm farm. The farm image is for demonstration purposes only.

Dead palms are replaced, on average 200 days after they die.

At 1× a year takes about 15 s (24 days a second). Switching replanting or traps keeps the current day. Reset (or Home) returns every film to day 1. Space plays or pauses; the arrow keys step a day, Shift + arrow a week.

What each frame shows

  • Palms: the crowns in the image stand for the susceptible palms; amber cryptic (infested, no symptoms yet), red symptomatic, black dead.
  • Blue cloud: females flying over the last 7 days, log scale 0.01–10 per palm.
  • Navy outline: a palm releasing at least one flying female that day.
  • Rings: black, infested that day; grey and fading, infested in the previous 6 days.
  • White outline: the farm boundary. Palms outside it are simulated but not drawn.
  • Strip: the whole orchard's females flying and new infestations per week for the current year, red cursor at the day.
  • Traps (when shown): green triangles at six random points, the same in both simulations, and chosen so that all six fall inside the picture (they are not the main page's six); dashed circles, each trap's 50 m catchment. The middle strip panel is the catch per trap per week, the mean of the six traps (bars: simulated counts; line: expected catch).

The simulated orchard is a regular 8 m grid laid over the planting. The image is not georeferenced: the grid was matched to the crowns on one block, so elsewhere a drawn palm can sit up to half a spacing off a real crown.

Palms die on average 9.3 months after infestation (median 8.9, 10–90 % 5–14 months): about 2 months to symptoms, then a collapse spread over four hidden stages. Fewer than 1 % die within 3 months. The first black palm appears in June of year 1 without replanting and in August with it: the fastest of the four introduced palms in each simulation.

The traps only watch: each one catches a small share of the females that land within 50 m of it, and the catches are not taken out of the population, so the spread is exactly the same with traps shown or hidden. The catch rate is a placeholder until it is fitted to field counts. Each trap and its whole circle had to fit inside the picture, which covers the middle of the orchard, so these six traps ended up close to where the infestation starts. They catch a few females from the first spring, while the infestation is still small, and the catch then grows year by year with the whole orchard's flying females (about 2–4 females per trap per week in year 6). On the main page, six traps spread at random over the whole orchard catch almost nothing for the first three years. How early traps see a new infestation depends strongly on where they stand relative to it.
